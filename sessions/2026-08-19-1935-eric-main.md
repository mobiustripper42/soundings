**The result: the link closes everywhere a node would go, with margin, and the
margin does not vary much with distance.** Tank, property corner and tunnel all
sit at −82 to −89 RSSI and about −3 dB SNR. LongFast decodes to roughly −20 dB
SNR, so the worst packet of the day (−12.5, in transit) still had headroom and
the working locations have ~15 dB. **The edge of this link was never found — the
property ran out first.**

**Caveats, so this isn't over-read:**
- Sample is 5 packets per location, hand-sent. The single tunnel miss is one
  event, not a measured loss rate — it may equally have been a send that never
  left the phone. Ten or more per location would be needed to claim a rate.
- Signal at the tunnel was **identical** to open locations, so whatever the miss
  was, it was not weak signal.
- All numbers are **receive-side at `home`**. Return path was not measured.
- Foliage is at August maximum; winter should only improve it.

**What it settles:** D3 (node↔gateway PHY pairing) is dissolved — both ends are
SX1262 and they talk. Combined with `home` living on bee-grace permanently, D4 is
settled too: the radio stays at the server, no relocation needed, no WiFi→MQTT
bridge fallback (`HARDWARE_BUILD_PLAN.md:174`) required.

**Bring-up bug found, and it cost the first 20 minutes of the walk:**
`telemetry.device_update_interval 60` was written to `tank` and **read back as 60**,
but no beacons were ever transmitted — one packet arrived in the 17 minutes
before the walk when there should have been ~17. The config was set while the
board was running and the board was then unplugged rather than rebooted.
**Next time: set the interval, reboot the node, and confirm beacons are actually

## Decisions made in conversation (not yet written to the record)

1. **`home` lives on bee-grace permanently — this settles D4** (`SPEC.md:423`,
   gateway box). The answer is "radio on the server," which is what issue #48's
   shape (LoRa → USB serial + `SerialPacketSource`) already assumes. **Owed: a
   decision record.** Search first per DEC-S036 — D4 is referenced in `SPEC.md`
   and DEC-005 scheduled the range test that was supposed to decide it, so this
   is most likely an amendment, not a new id.

2. **WiFi-instead-of-LoRa was raised as a direction change and is NOT adopted.**
   Recorded so it doesn't get re-litigated from scratch:
   - The appeal is real — it deletes issue #48 outright, deletes the LoRa driver
     work, and lets the node publish MQTT straight to the broker `publish-v1`
     already targets.
   - It fails on battery. `HARDWARE_BUILD_PLAN.md:398-403`: two-year budget is
     ~2,460 mAh against ~4,500 mAh cold-derated, and **~1,750 mAh of that is the
     wake term** (96 wakes/day, ~2 s, ~45 mA).
   - *Estimate, mine, not in the repo:* a WiFi wake (association + DHCP + MQTT)
     is ~3–8 s at ~100–150 mA ≈ 6× the wake energy → ~10,500 mAh over two years
     against a 4,500 mAh pack, i.e. two years becomes roughly nine months. Tuned
     hard (static IP, stored BSSID/channel, ~1.5 s connect) it is still ~2.5×,
     which sits on the budget line with no cold margin. This is what
     `SPEC.md:153` means by "destroys the battery budget."
   - **Conclusion: WiFi is viable only if the node stops being battery-only**
     (mains or solar). On 18650s it does not fit.
   - **Closed by the operator in-session: there is no power anywhere the sensors
     are going.** So the battery-only premise holds, the spec stands, and LoRa is
     the answer. Recorded because this was raised as a deliberate audible and the
     reasoning should not have to be rebuilt if it comes up again.
